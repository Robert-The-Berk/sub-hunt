Sonar log

    How to play
    • Active buoys (amber): drop one on a square and it takes a turn to deploy, then pings every turn for 5 turns. Its ring shows faint until the first ping. If the sub is within 10 nmi (5 squares) of it, you may get a return showing its range as a ring. Big subs echo reliably; small ones often don't.
    • The sub hears pings much further away than you can hear echoes: out to 24 nmi (12 squares, the faint outer ring when you aim). When it hears one it speeds up and steers to stay out of that buoy's range, so drop them where it has to go, not where it is.
    • No return doesn't prove the sub isn't there. A small sub can sit inside the circle and still not echo.
    • Sonobuoys (teal) listen silently every turn up to 16 nmi away and report a bearing, not a range. Fast subs are loud; slow ones are hard to hear.
    • The sub is smart: it hears a buoy splash within about 8 nmi (less if it is deep), remembers where it went in and steers around it, so drop buoys well ahead of it or off its track. It weaves toward the escape line, changes its escape point, and comes at the convoy from a flank. When spooked, quiet boats usually go silent and creep off; loud ones sprint in short bursts.
    • Thermal layer: the ocean has a layer that bends sound. Each buoy is set shallow or deep (key D). Across the layer, passive buoys hear only about a third as well, and active pings reach only 6 nmi with a weaker echo. The sub picks a side and may slip across the layer when it is pinged, and a nearby buoy may hear its hull pop as it changes depth. Shallow buoys hear the convoy loudly; deep ones much less.
    • Sea life: whales, dolphins and snapping shrimp make noise too. Each sounds different in the log and looks different on the waterfall: whale song comes and goes in broad bands, dolphins flicker and move fast, shrimp crackle steadily near rocks, and a sperm whale clicks in a broken line. Whales can even return an echo. With Jonesy on watch, he names them.
    • Decoys: when a ping catches it or a charge lands close, the sub may drop a noisemaker and creep away deep and slow. A decoy echoes like a hull, but passive buoys hear broadband noise with no blade rate, its waterfall trace is broad and smeared, and it barely moves. A short mechanical transient on a buoy can mean one was just launched.
    • Sonar waterfall: shows what one passive buoy hears, bearing across and time going down. A steady line is something making noise. It shows the sub even when it is too faint for a logged contact, so look for a weak line that keeps the same bearing turn after turn. Small marks at the top show where the friendly ships are. Click a line to plot that bearing on the map.
    • Depth charges (red) hit a 3×3 area where the sub is now, before it moves. You can't drop one on top of a friendly ship.
    • The convoy (white hulls) sails east along the dashed route. The sub will close to within 8 nmi and fire torpedoes. A launch is loud: the targeted ship reports a red bearing and you can see the torpedo run, so you get one turn to answer. A sub that has just been pinged or charged is more cautious about firing.
    • You lose if every ship is sunk, if you run out of charges, or if the sub reaches the escape line on the west edge.
    • Plot pencil (key 4): mark where you think the sub is and draw its likely course. It's free and doesn't use a turn. Guess marks show the turn you made them, so a row of them traces a course.
    • Every action takes one turn. After each turn the sub moves.
    • Debrief: each hunt is scored for sinking the sub, ships saved, speed and gear left over, with bonuses for using few active buoys and for not wasting charges on decoys. Up to 3 stars, and your best score for each target is saved.
    • Campaign: five missions drawing from one supply pool. Gear you don't use goes back in the pool, and each win adds a small resupply. You can retry a failed mission, but the gear you spent is gone. Run out of depth charges and the campaign is over.
    • When the hunt ends you can watch a replay of the sub's course, your buoys and every contact, turn by turn.
