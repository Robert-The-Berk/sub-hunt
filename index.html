Sonar log

    How to play
    • Active buoys (amber): drop one on a square and it pings every turn for 4 turns. If the sub is within 10 nmi (5 squares) of it, you may get a return showing its range as a ring. Big subs echo reliably; small ones often don't.
    • The sub hears pings much further away than you can hear echoes: out to 24 nmi (12 squares, the faint outer ring when you aim). When it hears one it speeds up and steers to stay out of that buoy's range, so drop them where it has to go, not where it is.
    • No return doesn't prove the sub isn't there. A small sub can sit inside the circle and still not echo.
    • Sonobuoys (teal) listen silently every turn up to 16 nmi away and report a bearing, not a range. Fast subs are loud; slow ones are hard to hear.
    • Sonar waterfall: shows what one passive buoy hears, bearing across and time going down. A steady line is something making noise. It shows the sub even when it is too faint for a logged contact, so look for a weak line that keeps the same bearing turn after turn. Small marks at the top show where the friendly ships are. Click a line to plot that bearing on the map.
    • Depth charges (red) hit a 3×3 area where the sub is now, before it moves. You can't drop one on top of a friendly ship.
    • The convoy (white hulls) sails east along the dashed route. The sub will close to within 8 nmi and fire torpedoes. A launch is loud: the targeted ship reports a red bearing and you can see the torpedo run, so you get one turn to answer. A sub that has just been pinged or charged is more cautious about firing.
    • You lose if every ship is sunk, if you run out of charges, or if the sub reaches the escape line on the west edge.
    • Plot pencil (key 4): mark where you think the sub is and draw its likely course. It's free and doesn't use a turn. Guess marks show the turn you made them, so a row of them traces a course.
    • Every action takes one turn. After each turn the sub moves.
    • When the hunt ends you can watch a replay of the sub's course, your buoys and every contact, turn by turn.
